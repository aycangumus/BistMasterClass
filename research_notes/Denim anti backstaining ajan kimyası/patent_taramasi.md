## 1. Which patents claim polymeric anti-backstaining agents?

### Takeaway
Five Western patent families and at least four Chinese ones claim polymers specifically for indigo backstaining in denim wet processing. The recurring architectures are (a) water-dispersible aromatic polyester–polyether (terephthalate/isophthalate + PEG or alkoxylated polyol: Seydel 1998 and 2017, Sasol 2003), (b) hydrophobically modified polycarboxylates (National Starch 1999; styrene–maleic anhydride hydrolysate in Chinese filings 2011–2017), (c) the Rohm and Haas 1993 quartet (polycarboxylic dispersant, acrylamide polymer, aryl sulfonic acid condensate, associative thickener) and (d) N-vinylpyrrolidone-containing polymers (PVP K-30 as co-component; NVP/fumaric/acrylic copolymer esterified with fatty alcohol). No patent was found that claims PVP-VI, PVNO, PEI alkoxylates, cellulose ethers or PEG-g-vinyl acetate graft copolymers specifically for denim; National Starch explicitly states that conventional dye-transfer inhibitors (PVP, PVNO) are not effective against indigo.

### Inferences
- The two independent polyester families (Seydel: recycled-PET/isophthalate/trimellitate + alkoxylated polyol; Sasol: >90 mol % terephthalate + EG + PEG 2,000–8,000) are structurally the same idea as laundry PET-POET soil-release polymers. The Sasol text's emphasis on non-cotton pocket linings, labels and zippers suggests the aromatic polyester block is there to adsorb on polyester parts while PEG provides the hydrated barrier. This is my reading; the retrieved passages do not state the mechanism.
- Seydel's trimellitic component (3–15 %) most likely supplies free carboxyl groups for water dispersibility (anionic character); again not stated in the retrieved text.
- Carboxylated polymers carrying an aromatic or alkyl hydrophobe (National Starch hydrophobically modified poly-acid; SMA hydrolysate in CN 102392374 and CN 107385976) form a second consistent design family: anionic backbone for dispersion plus hydrophobe for binding indigo particles.
- PVP appears only as a minor co-component (2–10 %) or as a comonomer, never as the sole claimed agent, which is consistent with National Starch's statement that PVP/PVNO alone do not work on indigo.
- The Dow Corning granule is primarily a softener delivery form (aminosilicone on zeolite) claimed to reduce backstaining; the polyacrylic acid binder and zeolite may contribute dispersing/adsorbing effects, but the patent text retrieved does not apportion the effect.

## 2. Which patents claim surfactant-based agents?

### Takeaway
No patent was found whose independent claim is a surfactant alone as the denim anti-backstaining agent; in every retrieved record surfactants are co-components of polymer or enzyme systems, and the enzyme patents' background sections state that surfactant addition alone is "not entirely satisfactory". The surfactants actually exemplified are an octylphenol ethoxylate (Triton X-120, Genencor), fatty alcohol ethoxylates (AEO-3, AEO-9, Peregal O-100, tallow alcohol ethoxylate), fatty amine ethoxylates (grade "1830"), coconut diethanolamide, and aryl sulfonic acid condensates (Rohm and Haas; dispersant NNO as a negative comparator).

### Cited Findings
- Genencor (WO 94/29426 / EP 0 702 713 B1 / US 6,251,144): the exemplified backstain-inhibiting blend contains redepositing Trichoderma cellulase, subtilisin protease GC399 and the nonionic surfactant Triton X-120; the examples conclude that adding protease to a redepositing cellulase in the presence of surfactant markedly reduces redeposition — [WO1994029426A1](https://patents.google.com/patent/WO1994029426A1/en); [EP0702713B1](https://patents.google.com/patent/EP0702713B1/en)
- Background of the Genencor and Novozymes families: earlier attempts added extra anti-redeposition chemicals such as surfactants to the cellulase wash to disperse loosened indigo, but these methods "are not entirely satisfactory and some objectionable backstaining remains" — [USPTO PDF US 6,251,144](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/6251144); [Justia US 6,797,010](https://patents.justia.com/patent/6797010)
- Iogen (US 5,700,686 / US 5,916,799): in the examples, adding surfactant to the protease-treated cellulase further reduces backstaining without significant loss of net dye release — [USPTO PDF US 5,916,799](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/5916799); [USPTO PDF US 5,700,686](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/5700686)

## 3. Which patents claim enzymatic or protein-based solutions?

### Takeaway
The enzyme literature is the largest block and splits into four strategies: (i) adding protease to a redepositing Trichoderma cellulase or pre-treating the cellulase with protease (Genencor 1993; Iogen mid-1990s), (ii) engineering or selecting cellulases that bind indigo less (EG III-rich, truncated cores lacking the cellulose-binding domain, neutral Chrysosporium/Humicola-type endoglucanases, fusion proteins), (iii) adding a second enzyme that releases the redeposited dye (cutinase/lipase for polyester-containing pocket parts, Novozymes 2000; pectinase, Novo Nordisk 1998), and (iv) GH61 auxiliary polypeptides (Novozymes 2010). Only the Genencor protease family yielded a numeric result here: back-side reflectance 39.07 for cellulase alone versus 42.87 with added protease.

- For a formulation chemist the practical consequence is that any chemical anti-backstaining agent must not denature or inhibit the cellulase (the stated selection criterion in CN 102392374) and that its benefit will depend on which cellulase is used (acid Trichoderma vs neutral endoglucanase).

## 4. Which patents use inorganic/particulate scavengers or sequestrants?

### Takeaway
Only one patent with a particulate component was found: Dow Corning's US 8,618,039, where at least 40 wt % zeolite (type 4A preferred) is the carrier of an aminosilicone/polyacrylic acid granule that is claimed to minimise backstaining. Clay adsorption of indigo is documented in academic papers (montmorillonite nanoclay), not in the patents retrieved; no patent was found for bentonite, perlite, silica, activated carbon or sequestrants as indigo scavengers.

### Cited Findings
- US 8,618,039 B2: granule with at least 40 wt % aluminosilicate carrier (zeolite; 40–90 parts), 5–25 parts nitrogen-functional silicone, 5–40 parts polyacrylic acid binder; added to the enzymatic or pumice bath, it "helps prevent redeposition of indigo dyes on fabric" — [US8618039B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US8618039B2/en)
- Academic (not patent): montmorillonite-based nanoclay particles adsorb released indigo from the wash liquor; combining laccase and montmorillonite gave lower indigo staining on the garment than conventional bio-washing — [Journal of Cleaner Production article](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0959652614013031?via=ihub)
- Academic (not patent): nanoclay addition reduced backstaining because of indigo adsorption on the clay — [ResearchGate: Nanoclay as new tool for discoloration of dyed denim garment with indigo](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/257815304_Nanoclay_as_New_Tool_for_Discoloration_of_Dyed_Denim_Garment_with_Indigo)
- CN 107385976 enzyme powder contains 10–80 parts "filler" and 5–30 parts buffer as solid carriers; the filler identity was not returned — [CN107385976A](https://patents.google.com/patent/CN107385976A/zh)

### Inferences
- In the patent record, inorganic solids appear as carriers for granulated actives rather than as claimed indigo scavengers; the scavenger concept seems to live in the academic literature and in supplier practice more than in claims.
- The Dow Corning granule shows that a cationic-leaning softener (aminosilicone) can be combined with an anionic polymer binder and zeolite without worsening backstaining, which is relevant when softening and abrasion are run in one bath.

### Gaps
- No patents retrieved for bentonite, perlite, silica, kaolin or activated carbon as indigo adsorbents in the wash bath, nor for sequestrants (phosphonates, polyphosphates, EDTA/citrate) claimed for backstaining. Only one query was run on this point.
- Whether Dow Corning's examples isolate the contribution of zeolite versus silicone versus binder is unknown.

## 6. What structural features do the patents themselves say are essential for performance?

### Takeaway
The explicit statements retrieved are few: (1) indigo is extremely hydrophobic, so hydrophilic dye-complexing polymers (PVP, PVNO) fail and a hydrophobic moiety on a hydrophilic backbone is required (National Starch); (2) a hydrophobic anchor block that covers the indigo particle plus a hydrated EO chain giving steric/entropic stabilisation (block-polyether passage in a Chinese filing); (3) the agent must not depress cellulase activity (Soochow University, with naphthalene sulfonate NNO as the counter-example); and (4) non-cotton trims (polyester pocket linings, labels, zippers) need to be protected, which the polyester patents and the cutinase patent both address. No retrieved passage invokes hydrogen bonding to the indigo N–H or π–π stacking explicitly.

### Cited Findings
- Hydrophobicity argument: conventional anti-dye-transfer polymers (PVP, PVNO) work for direct dyes on cotton but "are not effective in preventing the backstaining of indigo dyes due to the extreme hydrophobicity of indigo dyes"; the remedy claimed is a polymer with a hydrophilic backbone and a hydrophobic moiety — [USPTO PDF US 6,537,327](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/6537327); [USPTO PDF US 6,337,313](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/6337313)
- Hydrophobe source and type: from a hydrophobic comonomer or a chain-transfer agent, including alkyl-aryl / aryl-alkyl groups, on a backbone of unsaturated C1–C6 acid monomers — [USPTO PDF US 6,337,313](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/6337313)
- Anchor-and-stabiliser model: PO segments of a block polyether anchor to indigo and form a covering layer; EO segments extend into the water phase as a hydration film; steric hindrance and entropic repulsion keep the dye dispersed and reduce back-staining (attribution to the invention vs. prior art uncertain) — [CN107385976A](https://patents.google.com/patent/CN107385976A/zh)
- Suspension concept: the Seydel resin keeps released dye suspended in the bath for the duration of the treating step so that it does not settle back on the fabric — [USPTO PDF US 6,008,182](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/6008182)
- Aromatic polyester + polyether composition is essential in both polyester families: more than 90 mol % terephthalic acid with EG and PEG 2,000–8,000 (Sasol); terephthalate/isophthalate/trimellitate with polyols carrying 5–30 EO or PO per hydroxyl (Seydel) — [US20080028539A1](https://patents.google.com/patent/US20080028539A1/en); [USPTO PDF US 6,008,182](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/6008182)
- Protection of non-cotton trims is an explicit goal: pocket linings, seams, labels and zippers "which often are not made of cotton" (Sasol); backstaining of pocket parts (Novozymes cutinase) — [FreePatentsOnline US 2008/0028539](https://www.freepatentsonline.com/y2008/0028539.html); [Justia US 6,797,010](https://patents.justia.com/patent/6797010)
- Enzyme compatibility as a selection criterion: the SMA/PVP/ethoxylate agent has little effect on acid cellulase activity whereas dispersant NNO strongly reduces it — [CN102392374B](https://patents.google.com/patent/CN102392374B/en)
- Penetration aid: small-molecule surfactants are added so that the enzyme liquor penetrates the fabric, improving enzyme wash and lowering back-staining — [CN103233377A](https://patents.google.com/patent/CN103233377A/en)

### Inferences
- Taken together the patents describe an amphiphilic dispersant requirement: a hydrophobic or aromatic segment with affinity for indigo particles (and, for the polyesters, for polyester trim) combined with a strongly hydrated nonionic (PEG/EO) or anionic (carboxylate) segment. Styrene units (SMA), terephthalate/isophthalate units and alkyl-aryl hydrophobes are all aromatic, which is compatible with a π-stacking rationale, but the patents retrieved argue only in terms of hydrophobicity.
- Pyrrolidone units (PVP K-30, NVP comonomer) are present in two Chinese formulations and could act as hydrogen-bond acceptors for the indigo N–H; the patents retrieved give no such explanation, so this remains a hypothesis for the report writer to label as such.
- Low foaming, HLB windows and specific molecular-weight ranges for the polycarboxylates were not found in any retrieved claim; only the PEG block (2,000–8,000 g/mol, Sasol) and alkoxylation degree (5–30 mol per OH, Seydel) are numerically specified.
