## 1. What are the standard and commonly used test methods for backstaining and anti-backstaining performance? Which supplier in-house methods are published?

### Takeaway
There is no dedicated ISO/AATCC "backstaining" standard in the sources found; the field uses (i) reflectance/CIELAB readings on the reverse side of the denim and on white pocket/lining fabric, (ii) K/S of a white cotton swatch washed with the denim, and (iii) small-scale "microassays" with an indigo suspension + white cotton + enzyme. The most explicit protocol descriptions are in enzyme-supplier patents (Novo/Novozymes, Genencor, the Emalfarb/C1 family, the AB Enzymes-type fusion-cellulase family), not in standards.

### Cited Findings

**Garment/panel-scale instrumental colour measurement**
- Two-wavelength reflectometer method on washed jeans: reflectance at **680 nm on the outside** and at **420 nm on the inside**; trials run in a **semi-industrial washing machine** (not a Launder-Ometer); C1 (Chrysosporium) cellulase benchmarked against Novo's neutral cellulases Denimax 700T / Denimax L and Genencor's acid cellulase Primafast 100. Reported outcome: C1 reflectance similar to Denimax L at both wavelengths and "significantly better" than Primafast 100 at both 680 and 420 nm (numeric Table 16 n/r). — [US 5,811,381 (inventor M. A. Emalfarb)](https://patents.google.com/patent/US5811381A/en); [US 6,015,707](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/6015707); [WO 98/15633](https://patents.google.com/patent/WO1998015633A1/en)
- Direction-of-scale warning for that method: one snippet reads "the higher the signal detected at 680 nm (outside of the jeans), the lower the backstaining" and the same for 420 nm inside; another snippet from the same family states that for the back-side reading "the higher value the more backstaining". The two readings of the patent text conflict and must be checked against the full patent before use. — [US 5,811,381](https://patents.google.com/patent/US5811381A/en); [US 6,015,707 (freepatentsonline)](https://www.freepatentsonline.com/6015707.html)
- CIELAB b* on the reverse side: backstaining measured **on the reverse side of denim panels** with a **Datacolor Texflash 2000 reflectometer, D65**, reported on the CIELAB **(−b\*)** coordinate; in the same document the abrasion "look" is defined by reflection at **420 nm** on the face. — [WO 99/51808, "Treatment of denim fabric with a pectolytic enzyme"](https://patents.google.com/patent/WO1999051808A1/en); related listing [US 6,146,428 "Enzymatic treatment of denim"](https://patents.justia.com/patent/6146428)
- "L (right)" / "b (reverse)" convention: parameter **L measured on the right (face) side** = lightening/abrasion; **b measured on the reverse side** = blueing of the reverse side, i.e. backstaining. — [US 6,723,549 "Cellulases, the genes encoding them and uses thereof" family](https://patents.google.com/patent/US6723549B2/en) (the search summary did not state unambiguously which patent in the result set carries this wording; this family is the likely one)
- Face + reverse + pocket, before/after: colour as reflectance with a **Minolta CM 2500 spectrophotometer, L\*a\*b\*, D65/2°**; colour of the **face side, the reverse side and the pockets** measured after desizing and again after cellulase treatment. — [WO 2007/118935 "Enzyme fusion proteins and their use"](https://patents.google.com/patent/WO2007118935A1/en); [CA 2648926](https://patents.google.com/patent/CA2648926A1/en)
- HunterLab variant: average **L\*** (higher L\* = more abrasion) and average **b\*** (more negative b\*, "bluer" = more backstaining). — [US 7,041,488 "Endo-beta-1,4-glucanase from Bacillus"](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/7041488)
- † Genencor patents compute dye removal and backstaining from reflectance/brightness readings and caution that reflectance values track redeposition on the back side of the garment but do not accurately describe the blue/white fibre contrast on the abraded face. — [US 6,251,144 (Genencor; Kellis, Poulose, Yoon)](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/6251144)
- A denim-bleaching patent states that **visual evaluation is a better method of determining back-staining than the standard ISO/CIE colour-code method**. — [US 5,749,923](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/5749923)

**K/S on a white adjacent swatch**
- A 10 cm × 10 cm **white lining cloth is added to the wash**; afterwards the **K/S of the white cloth at the dye's maximum-absorption wavelength** is measured; lower K/S = better anti-backstaining. Standard test recipe in that document: acid cellulase 2 % o.w.f., pH 5.1, anti-backstaining agent 1 g/L, liquor ratio 15:1, 50 °C, 60 min. — [CN102392374B (Soochow University; filed 2011-07-07, granted 2013-07-10)](https://patents.google.com/patent/CN102392374B/en)
- K/S of the washed denim itself used as a (contrast/back-staining) response in a Box–Behnken design; "higher K/S value" interpreted as "less contrast / higher back-staining". — [Patra, Madhu & Bala, Fashion and Textiles 5 (2018)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40691-017-0126-9)

**Model systems / microassays (indigo suspension + white cotton + enzyme)**
- Gusakov, Sinitsyn, Berlin et al. (1998): "a model microassay system … to measure indigo backstaining on cotton fabrics in the presence of enzymes on a small laboratory scale"; **backstaining indexes** determined for **11 cellulase samples**, which were ranked from lower to higher backstaining (index values n/r). — [Appl. Biochem. Biotechnol. 75, 279–293 (1998)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF02787781)
- Sinitsyn, Gusakov, Grishutin, Sinitsyna, Ankudimova (2001): abrasion and backstaining microassays run at test-tube scale, at **20–30 mL or 2–3 mL**; **29 cellulase preparations** compared for abrasive activity; four purified *Chrysosporium lucknowense* cellulases assayed. — [J. Biotechnol. 89(2-3), 233–238 (2001), PMID 11500217](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11500217/); secondary summary on [ResearchGate author page](https://www.researchgate.net/profile/Alexander-Gusakov)
- Protocol details reproduced in a later patent: the standard assay uses **3.5 × 3.5 cm swatches in 2–3 repetitions and 2–5 mg protein**; the minimised microassay needs **0.2–0.5 mg protein**; an indigo suspension is made by stirring **250 mg indigo in 50 mL distilled water (5 g/L stock, "Method B")**; evaluation by image processing; a *T. reesei* preparation (ACE) is the **high-backstaining internal standard** and IndiAge Super GX the **low-backstaining second internal standard**. — [US 7,923,236 "Fungal enzymes"](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/7923236)
- A companion methods paper, "Microassays to control the results of cellulase treatment of denim fabrics", Textile Chemist and Colorist (2000), is cited as the textile-journal description of the same assays (not retrieved). — [review chapter listing](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-981-10-2474-0_3)
- Indigo-binding assays: adsorption of cellulase protein onto insoluble indigo particles, and binding of indigo to amino acids immobilised on cross-linked agarose (Tyr and Phe bind best). — [Gusakov et al., Biocatalysis-2000 paper (MSU)](https://org321-5.chem.msu.ru/eng/journals/vmgu/00add/77.pdf); [J. Biotechnol. 2001 record](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0168165601002346)

**Lab-machine protocols**
- VTT/Röhm screening of *Melanocarpus albomyces* cellulases used a **Launder-Ometer method at pH 7** for biostoning/backstaining evaluation. — [Miettinen-Oinonen et al., Enzyme Microb. Technol. 34, 332–341 (2004)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S014102290300365X)
- † Heikinheimo et al. (2000) evaluated denim swatches by reflectance units and by a visual panel, and also measured reducing sugars and the absorbance/lightness of the treatment liquors. — [VTT record, Textile Research Journal 2000](https://cris.vtt.fi/en/publications/treating-denim-fabrics-with-itrichoderma-reeseii-cellulases/)

**Indigo particle size as a measured variable**
- Smaller indigo particles gave higher backstaining on cotton (attributed to larger specific surface area) in a model study with acid cellulase. — [Hao et al., Adv. Mater. Res. 798–799, 35–38 (2013)](https://www.scientific.net/AMR.798-799.35)
- Cellulase proteins are reported to reduce indigo particle size and act as carriers of fine indigo particles to cotton. — [Campos et al., "Indigo–Cellulase Interactions", TRJ 70(6) 532–536 (2000), ResearchGate record](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/220030789_Indigo-Cellulase_Interactions)

### Inferences
- The de-facto "standard" in supplier R&D is a two-number readout: face-side lightness or reflectance (L\*, or R at 420 nm) for abrasion, plus reverse-side b\* (or reverse/pocket L\*) for backstaining. Academic groups more often use K/S of a white co-washed swatch or the microassay "backstaining index".
- Sign conventions are not harmonised (higher R = less staining; more negative b\* = more staining; higher K/S = more staining; and one patent family is internally ambiguous), so numbers cannot be pooled across sources without re-deriving direction.
- Published supplier methods located: Novo/Novozymes-type (Texflash, b\* reverse, R420 face), Genencor (reflectance/brightness, dye removal vs backstaining), Emalfarb/C1 (680/420 nm), fusion-cellulase/AB Enzymes-type (Minolta CM 2500, face/reverse/pocket). None is a formal standard.

These designations come from the task brief/background knowledge only; no source was retrieved showing them applied to denim backstaining.
- No source found describing Linitest, Terg-O-Tometer, filter tests, turbidity, sedimentation/dispersion-stability tests, DLS/laser-diffraction sizing or zeta potential applied specifically to wash-liquor indigo; a dedicated search returned only dyeing/effluent/pigment papers ([example: indigo dispersant study, J. Text. Res. 2022](http://www.fzxb.org.cn/EN/Y2022/V43/I04/84)).
- No source found for bath-indigo quantification by extraction (DMSO/chloroform/NMP) or dithionite reduction in a backstaining context.
- Full protocols (load, liquor ratio, steel balls, swatch size) for Novozymes Launder-Ometer and AB Enzymes washer tests were not retrieved; assignee of WO 99/51808 and WO 2007/118935 not confirmed in-session.

## 2. How are abrasion level and backstaining separated?

### Takeaway
By measuring different surfaces/coordinates (face L\* or R420 for abrasion; reverse-side b\*/L\*, pocket, or white swatch K/S for redeposition) and, in the better studies, by comparing enzymes at roughly equal dye removal rather than equal dose.

### Cited Findings
- Face vs reverse split: L on the right side = abrasion; b on the reverse side = backstaining. — [US 6,723,549 family](https://patents.google.com/patent/US6723549B2/en); same logic with L\*/b\* in [US 7,041,488](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/7041488)
- Face R420 nm for the abraded look, reverse (−b\*) for backstaining, in one protocol. — [WO 99/51808](https://patents.google.com/patent/WO1999051808A1/en)
- Equal-abrasion comparison: denim treated with protease-treated cellulase "exhibits much less backstaining than the redepositing cellulase **at roughly the same degree of dye removal**" (their Table 3; numbers n/r). — [US 5,700,686 (Genencor)](https://patents.google.com/patent/US5700686A/en); [US 5,916,799](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/5916799)
- Separate surfaces analysed separately: Cavaco-Paulo et al. "analyzed dye staining levels on different cotton surfaces" during enzyme stone washing. — [TRJ 68(6), 398–401 (1998)](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/004051759806800602)
- Separate microassays for abrasive activity and for backstaining, run on the same enzyme set. — [Sinitsyn et al. 2001](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11500217/)
- Garment studies of the Montazer/Maryan group measure colour indices on the **front, the back and the white pocket** of the garment. — [J. Cleaner Prod. 57, 320–326 (2013) record](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S095965261300379X)
- † Limitation acknowledged by a supplier: back-side reflectance does not capture face-side blue/white contrast. — [US 6,251,144](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/6251144)

### Inferences
- A study that reports only face-side L\* or K/S of the denim (e.g. the Box–Behnken K/S design) confounds abrasion with redeposition; only reverse-side, pocket or white-swatch readings isolate backstaining.

### Gaps
- No source retrieved that defines a numeric "backstaining index" formula (the Gusakov index definition is in the paywalled full text).
- No explicit "iso-abrasion" plots (reverse b\* vs face L\*) could be extracted.

## 3. What do the comparative studies find? (study-by-study records; all abstract/snippet level)

### Takeaway
The consistent findings are: acid *Trichoderma* cellulases backstain more than neutral *Humicola*/Cel45-type or alkaline endoglucanases; protease (in-bath, in rinse, or as protease-treated cellulase) and ethoxylated nonionic surfactants cut redeposition; laccase, nano-clay and ozone reduce reverse/pocket staining by destroying or sequestering indigo. Direct head-to-head numeric comparisons of commercial anti-backstaining chemistries (nonionics vs PVP vs polyacrylate vs CMC) are almost absent from the accessible literature — one paper (Zilz et al. 2013) is the only clear one found.

### Cited Findings

**3.1 Cellulase type, structure, dose**
- Cavaco-Paulo, Morgado, Almeida, Kilburn (1998), TRJ 68(6):398–401, DOI 10.1177/004051759806800602. Method: staining levels on different cotton surfaces during enzyme stone washing. Conclusion: the high ability of cellulase protein to bind to cotton cellulose is the major cause of backstaining. Fabric/recipe/numbers n/r. — [SAGE](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/004051759806800602); [Minho repository](https://repositorium.sdum.uminho.pt/handle/1822/5616); [Scilit](https://scilit.com/publications/712a8163e06aa679363054e5a2bf7058)
- Andreaus, Campos, Gübitz, Cavaco-Paulo (2000), TRJ 70(7):628–632, DOI 10.1177/004051750007000711. Findings: increasing concentrations of fungal cellulases on the fabric **decrease** indigo staining; deleting the cellulose-binding domain lowers staining versus the intact enzyme; Family I CBDs reduce staining as their concentration rises, Family II CBDs have no effect. Numbers n/r. — [SAGE](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/004051750007000711); [Scilit abstract](https://scilit.com/publications/b5199fcfc80a345c614a543eb9ce8315)
- Campos, Andreaus, Gübitz, Cavaco-Paulo (2000), "Indigo–Cellulase Interactions", TRJ 70(6):532–536. Adsorption of cellulases of different fungal origin onto insoluble indigo: *T. reesei* "acid" cellulases bind indigo more strongly than *H. insolens* "neutral" cellulases. Numbers n/r. — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/220030789_Indigo-Cellulase_Interactions); [Springer index record](https://rd.springer.com/article/10.1385/ABAB%3A88%3A1-3%3A119)
- Review of the Minho work: dye–cellulase interaction, besides dye–cellulose interaction, is the major cause; whole enzymes show more indigo affinity and more backstaining than CBD-free cores; pH of maximum activity need not equal pH of maximum binding. — [Electron. J. Biotechnol. 6(3), 2003](https://www.scielo.cl/fbpe/img/ejb/v6n3/a08/bip/)
- Gusakov et al. (1998): protein adsorption on cotton is the crucial parameter for backstaining, for crude preparations and purified components alike (11 samples ranked). — [Appl. Biochem. Biotechnol. 75:279–293](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF02787781)
- Gusakov et al. (2000), Enzyme Microb. Technol. 27(9):664–671: six purified fungal cellulases (4 endoglucanases, 2 cellobiohydrolases) compared in the microassay; cellulases with hydrophobic surface clusters (Tyr/Phe/Trp) bind indigo and "act as emulsifiers helping the dye to float out". — [MSU conference version](https://org321-5.chem.msu.ru/eng/journals/vmgu/00add/77.pdf); [review listing](https://ouci.dntb.gov.ua/works/42zJkYm4)
- Sinitsyn et al. (2001): among four purified *C. lucknowense* enzymes a ~25 kDa endoglucanase is the key enzyme for strong denim abrasion. — [PubMed 11500217](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11500217/)
- Heikinheimo, Buchert, Miettinen-Oinonen, Suominen (2000), TRJ: purified EG I, EG II, CBH I and two mixtures from engineered *T. reesei*; **EG II removes most colour at the lowest hydrolysis level; CBH I gives no stonewash effect even at high dose.** Backstaining figures n/r. — [VTT](https://cris.vtt.fi/en/publications/treating-denim-fabrics-with-itrichoderma-reeseii-cellulases/); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/249784686_Treating_Denim_Fabrics_with_Trichoderma_Reesei_Cellulases)
- Miettinen-Oinonen & Suominen (2002), Appl. Environ. Microbiol. 68(8):3956–3964: enzyme preparation with elevated EGII content gave an improved stonewashing effect. — [PMC124001](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC124001)
- Miettinen-Oinonen et al. (2004): *M. albomyces* supernatants biostone well at neutral pH with low backstaining; of three purified enzymes the 20 kDa endoglucanase performs on denim. VTT dissertation: recombinant Ma Cel45A preparation "caused considerably less backstaining than the acid cellulase product of *T. reesei*". — [Enzyme Microb. Technol. 34:332–341](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S014102290300365X); [VTT dissertation](https://cris.vtt.fi/en/publications/trichoderma-reesei-strains-for-production-of-cellulases-for-the-t/)
- † Host background matters at neutral pH: a *T. reesei* background with egl1 and egl2 deleted (ALKO3269) gives less backstaining than ALKO3268/ALKO3133 backgrounds; at higher 20K-cellulase dose the background has only minor importance. — [US 7,273,748](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/7273748)
- Anish, Rahman & Rao (2007), Biotechnol. Bioeng., DOI 10.1002/bit.21175: alkali-stable endoglucanase from alkalothermophilic *Thermomonospora* sp. used under alkaline and non-buffered conditions; higher abrasive activity with lower backstaining, comparable to commercial acid and neutral cellulases (qualitative; numbers n/r). — [Wiley](https://analyticalsciencejournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/bit.21175)
- *Penicillium echinulatum* cellulases removed more colour from denim and gave less indigo redeposition than commercial acid cellulases. — [TRJ paper record, ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/220030795_Application_of_cellulases_from_Acrophialophora_nainiana_and_Penicillium_echinulatum_in_textile_processing_of_cellulosic_fibres)
- Novo Nordisk patent (Onishi, Fich, Toft, Schülein; filed 1998-03-02, issued 1999-09-28): adding a particular cellulase "first component" to the stonewash bath reduces back-staining. Examples n/r. — [US 5,958,083](https://patents.google.com/patent/US5958083)
- Supplier guidance: backstaining on indigo denim is usually resolved by switching from standard acid to modified-acid or neutral cellulase. — [Cotton Incorporated ISP-1003](https://www.cottoninc.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/12/ISP-1003-Enzyme-Technology-for-Cotton-Products.pdf)
- "Use of cellulases and their effects on denim fabric properties" (journal/authors not confirmed): neutral cellulases gave higher lightness; raising enzyme amount **increased** back-staining; strength loss and back-staining are the main risks. — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/281258031_Use_of_cellulases_and_their_effects_on_denim_fabric_properties)

**3.2 Protease**
- Genencor WO 94/29426 / EP 0702713 (redepositing *Trichoderma* cellulase INDIAGE 44L + protease): reflectance **40.66 with protease added in the rinse vs 39.07 for the standard rinse without protease**; protease-treated garments read similar to a non-redepositing cellulase treatment. (Snippet partly garbled; wavelength, side and recipe n/r — verify in the table.) — [WO 94/29426](https://patents.google.com/patent/WO1994029426A1/en); [EP 0702713 B1](https://patents.google.com/patent/EP0702713B1/en)

- Genencor US 5,700,686 / 5,916,799: protease-treated and purified cellulase compositions give much less backstaining at about equal dye removal. — [US 5,700,686](https://patents.google.com/patent/US5700686A/en)
- Journal counterpart: Yoon M.-Y., McDonald H., Chu K., Garratt C. (2000), Textile Chemist and Colorist & American Dyestuff Reporter 32, 25–29 — citation confirmed in a review; article text n/r. — [Cellulases in the Textile Industry (Springer, 2007)](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/1-4020-5377-0_4)
- Independent confirmations: protease added during rinsing or at the end of the cellulase step significantly reduced backstaining and improved contrast (Minho review); protease and AEO-9 both reduced backstaining efficiently in a cotton model (Hao 2013). — [Electron. - A UFSC (Brazil) document states acid cellulases give unacceptable backstaining and that an engineered alkaline protease at pH 8–10 and low dosage addresses it. — [UFSC repository PDF](https://repositorio.ufsc.br/bitstream/handle/123456789/93001/266669.pdf?sequence=1&isAllowed=y)

**3.3 Surfactants, dispersants, polymers**
- Zilz, Rau, Budag, Scharf, Cavaco-Paulo, Andreaus, Coloration Technology 129:49–54 (issue dated Feb 2013; some records say 2012), DOI 10.1111/cote.12003. System: indigo adsorption on undyed cotton and treatment of cotton with *Hypocrea jecorina* (= *T. reesei*) cellulases in presence of surfactants/dispersants. Results: **indigo adsorption reduced by more than 75 % with ethoxylated nonionic surfactants at concentrations below 0.2 g/L**; weight loss after 120 min increased with nonionic surfactants and with polyvinylpyrrolidone; protein adsorption on fabric fell as additive concentration rose; **nonionic surfactants outperformed PVP**. — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/264452207_Nonionic_surfactants_and_dispersants_for_biopolishing_and_stonewashing_with_Hypocrea_jecorina_cellulases); [Wiley](http://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/cote.12003/pdf); [CORE](https://core.ac.uk/reader/55625270)
- Related statement on the same record page: ethoxylated linear fatty alcohols and a modified polyacrylic acid were the most efficient reagents at low concentration to **desorb** indigo from cotton (attribution to this paper vs an earlier Andreaus post-washing paper not resolved). — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/264452207_Nonionic_surfactants_and_dispersants_for_biopolishing_and_stonewashing_with_Hypocrea_jecorina_cellulases)
- Hao L.Y. et al. (2013): nonionic AEO-9 (fatty alcohol ethoxylate, 9 EO) reduces backstaining of acid cellulase systems on cotton. Numbers n/r. — [Adv. Mater. Res. 798–799:35](https://www.scientific.net/AMR.798-799.35)
- CN102392374B (Soochow Univ.): formulated anti-back-staining agent for acid-cellulase washing containing, among other components, polyvinylpyrrolidone 2–10 % (composition table fragmentary); claimed little effect on acid cellulase activity; K/S data per embodiment n/r. — [CN102392374B](https://patents.google.com/patent/CN102392374B/en)
- CN107385976A: styrene–maleic anhydride copolymer blended with tallow fatty alcohol polyoxyethylene ether (80 EO) for indigo anti-backstaining in enzyme washing; data in its Table 1 (n/r). — [CN107385976A](https://patents.google.com/patent/CN107385976A/zh)
- Novozymes lists surfactants (anionic and/or nonionic) and polymers "such as PVP, polyacrylate and polyacrylamide" as conventional stonewash additives, without comparative data. — [EP 1290129](https://patents.google.com/patent/EP1290129A1/en)
- † Supplier patent text (Genencor protease family or Novozymes cutinase patent): adding surfactant further reduces backstaining without greatly affecting net dye removal. — [US 5,916,799](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/5916799)
- Patra et al. **3.7 Oxidative clean-up and ozone**
- Ozone in water after desizing/biostoning: pocket fabrics were backstained during desizing and biostoning; subsequent ozone treatment decreased the backstaining and bleached the jeans without significant strength loss; indigo-only fabrics responded more than indigo + sulphur fabrics. ### Inferences
- Evidence converges on a protein-mediated mechanism (cellulase binds both cotton and indigo), which explains why chemistries that strip or block protein (protease, ethoxylated nonionics) beat simple dispersing polymers such as PVP in the one direct comparison found.
- For polyester-containing pocketing the relevant chemistry differs (lipase/cutinase), implying that a single "anti-backstaining" readout on cotton does not predict pocket results.
- Most garment-scale academic papers report direction only; the only extractable numbers were the >75 % adsorption reduction (<0.2 g/L nonionic) and the 40.66 vs 39.07 reflectance pair.

### Gaps
- Not retrieved: any table of L\*/b\*/K/S/ΔE/grey-scale values from any study; fabric specifications and full recipes for nearly all studies.
- No comparative study found (searches run or budget exhausted) for CMC, polyacrylate vs PVP head-to-head, perlite vs pumice, laser vs enzyme backstaining, sodium perborate/peroxide + detergent or reductive clearing after-wash sequences.
- Bangladeshi/Pakistani/Indian garment-wash papers located by title only, content n/r: [Effect of enzyme washing combined with pumice stone](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/265384559_Effect_of_Enzyme_Washing_Combined_With_Pumice_Stone_on_the_Physical_Mechanical_and_Color_Properties_of_Denim_Garments); [Bio-stoning (2024)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/378304448_Bio-stoning_Heighten_the_Appearance_of_Denim_Garment_through_Application_of_Cellulase_Enzyme); [Sustainable denim washing by process optimization](https://www.scirp.org/journal/paperinformation?paperid=119650); [Optimizing denim washing … reduce pumice consumption (2025)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/396273245_Optimizing_denim_washing_with_cellulase_enzymes_eco-friendly_method_to_reduce_pumice_consumption).

## 5. What gaps and contradictions exist in the experimental literature?

### Takeaway
The literature is mechanistically rich but methodologically fragmented: different surfaces, coordinates and sign conventions, few equal-abrasion comparisons, and almost no open, numeric head-to-head testing of commercial anti-backstaining chemistries.

### Cited Findings
- Contradiction on enzyme dose: increasing fungal cellulase concentration on the fabric **decreases** indigo staining ([Andreaus et al. 2000](https://scilit.com/publications/b5199fcfc80a345c614a543eb9ce8315)) versus more bound cellulase protein → **higher** backstaining ([Hao et al. 2013](https://www.scientific.net/AMR.798-799.35)) and more enzyme → more back-staining on denim ([ResearchGate record](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/281258031_Use_of_cellulases_and_their_effects_on_denim_fabric_properties)); protein adsorption named the crucial cause ([Gusakov 1998](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF02787781)).
- Contradiction on the role of indigo binding: cellulases that bind indigo are described both as carriers that deposit fine indigo on cotton ([Campos et al. record](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/220030789_Indigo-Cellulase_Interactions)) and as emulsifiers that help the dye float out ([Gusakov et al.](https://org321-5.chem.msu.ru/eng/journals/vmgu/00add/77.pdf)).
- "Neutral cellulases have no backstaining problem" ([Çukurova review](https://muhendislik.cu.edu.tr/tr/Dergi/(28_2_2013)/13.pdf)) versus († ) patent evidence that neutral-pH results depend on the enzyme background and dose ([US 7,273,748](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/7273748)) and that binding pH optimum differs from activity pH optimum ([EJB review](https://www.scielo.cl/fbpe/img/ejb/v6n3/a08/bip/)).
- Measurement validity disputed: († ) instrumental reverse-side reflectance misses face contrast ([US 6,251,144](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/6251144)); visual rating claimed superior to the ISO/CIE colour method ([US 5,749,923](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/5749923)).
- Protease trade-off unresolved quantitatively: backstaining benefit versus loss of cellulase activity ([US 6,251,144](https://patents.justia.com/patent/6251144)).
- Secondary sources disagree on whether the three-enzyme one-step process changes lightness ([J. Cleaner Prod. 2013 record](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S095965261300379X)).

### Inferences
- The dose contradiction is probably a model-system artefact (pre-adsorbed cellulase blocking sites on undyed fabric vs enzyme releasing and carrying indigo in a real denim bath); it cannot be settled from abstracts.
- Commercial anti-backstaining agents are tested as unnamed "commercial agent" or inside patents, so chemistry–performance relationships are not publicly benchmarked.

### Gaps
- No round-robin or standardised backstaining method; no agreed index.
- Missing variables in the accessible studies: water hardness, indigo particle size distribution in real liquors, pocketing fibre type, sulphur-bottomed/topped denims, and ozone/laser routes with reverse-side data.
- Unexecuted searches (budget): 2020–2026 studies, perlite, laser, CMC/polyacrylate, after-wash clean-up sequences, ISO/AATCC standard texts, Novozymes/AB Enzymes full test protocols. To continue, the hosts above must be allowed in the session network settings and the search budget raised.
