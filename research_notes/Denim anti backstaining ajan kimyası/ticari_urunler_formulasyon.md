# Commercial denim anti-backstaining agents: disclosed compositions, formulation recipes and laundry application conditions

> **Method and reliability note (read first).** Research date: 2026-11-19. In this session direct page retrieval (WebFetch) was blocked at network level for every host tried (solutions.cht.com, www.scribd.com, textiledetails.com, fr.slideshare.net, garmon-mirror.pages.dev, s.veneneo.workers.dev), and the shared web-search budget (200 calls per turn across all agents) ran out after ~45 queries of mine. **Every finding below therefore comes from search-engine result summaries/snippets of the cited URL, not from a full read of the document.** Wording in quotes is as relayed by the search tool. Numbers should be re-checked against the original TDS/SDS/patent before being used in a formulation or quoted in a final report. Items marked **[INFERENCE]** are my reading, not a statement of the source. Items marked **[NOT VERIFIED]** are suppliers/products named in the brief for which no public document could be found. No product name or CAS number has been invented; **no SDS with CAS-numbered ingredients and concentration ranges was retrieved for any denim anti-backstaining product** (see Gaps).

## 1. Which products exist and what do TDS/SDS/product pages say about their chemistry?

### Takeaway
About 45 named commercial anti-backstaining (ABS) / anti-redeposition products from ~20 suppliers could be documented; where chemistry is disclosed at all it falls into three families: (a) fatty-alcohol ethoxylate (+ polymer) blends, (b) non-ionic water-soluble/dispersible polyester (PET-polyether, increasingly from recycled PET) "blockers", and (c) anionic/non-ionic dispersant-detergent powders. Suppliers almost never disclose polymer identity, active content or CAS numbers publicly; the richest disclosures are Rudolf (chemical basis + 28 % active for one grade), Seydel (polyester basis, 100 % and 25 % forms), ETCC (fatty alcohol polyglycol ether base) and RSA Industries (polyester + fatty acid esters + emulsifiers).

### Inferences
- **[INFERENCE]** The "polyester blocker" (CHT), "100 % polyester" (ARM Kimya), "nonionic polyester" (Seydel), "water-dispersible polyester polymer blend" (Mega P560K), "recycled-PET based" (Rudolf UPCYCLE RNB, ARMAPOL RYC, Seyco Sperse 802, Seycote ASP) products are all the same technology family as the patents in Section 4: non-ionic, water-soluble/dispersible terephthalate–polyoxyalkylene (PET-POET) polyesters, i.e. the soil-release-polymer chemistry of detergents, here used to occupy hydrophobic sites on polyester pocketing/elastane and to disperse indigo. No supplier document retrieved states the exact monomer composition.
- **[INFERENCE]** "Combination product of backstaining blocker and dispersing agent" (CHT CLEAN-SMX 2 / CSM 2) and "fatty alcohol ethoxylates and polymers" (Rudolf DAB) indicate that the standard liquid ABS product is a two-function blend: (i) a polymer that adsorbs on fibre/pocket ("blocker") + (ii) a non-ionic surfactant/dispersant that keeps indigo suspended.
- **[INFERENCE]** The solid 100 % grades (Fortres Gems, Seycote SR-100/ASP, ARMAPOL NGR granules, Mega P560K) exist largely so that laundries and local enzyme compounders can dilute or dry-blend them; this matches the Chinese listings that dose the powder "as % of enzyme formulation".
- **[INFERENCE]** Rudolf's 28 % active, pH ≈ 4 liquid suggests typical liquid polyester-type ABS products are ~25–30 % active dispersions (cf. Seydel: SR-100 "dispersed into a 25 % product").

## 2. What chemical descriptions recur?

### Takeaway
Six descriptors recur across suppliers, patents and literature: (1) fatty alcohol ethoxylates / polyglycol ethers (often low-EO), (2) non-ionic water-soluble polyesters (PET-polyether, incl. recycled-PET), (3) carboxylate polymers (styrene–maleic anhydride hydrolysate, polyacrylate, acrylic/fumaric/NVP terpolymers), (4) PVP, (5) fatty amine ethoxylates as co-surfactant, and (6) phosphate/chelating components. Commercial ABS agents are overwhelmingly declared non-ionic; "anionic/non-ionic" appears mainly for powders and clean-up detergents.

### Cited Findings
- Trade description of the category: "anti back staining agents mostly comprise of three substances – dispersing agents, chelating agents, and emulsifying agents" — [Fibre2Fashion, J. Ganesh Prasad](https://www.fibre2fashion.com/industry-article/3414/a-novel-anti-back-staining-method-for-denim-garments-a-cost-effective-process).
- A further trade document describes ABS agents built from "polyglycol ether derivatives and polymer phosphates", which sequester metal ions and perform better in hard water — [Scribd "Antibackstainingagent"](https://www.scribd.com/document/483501496/Antibackstainingagent).
- Fatty alcohol ethoxylate basis is stated explicitly by Rudolf (RUCOGEN DAB: "fatty alcohol ethoxylates and polymers"; X-DAB "fatty alcohol base") and ETCC (Euro Antibak NBS: "fatty alcohol polyglycol ether" blended with surfactants, detergents, dispersants) — [Rudolf Italy](https://www.rudolf-group.it/en/products/textile-auxiliaries/denim/washing-desizing-stonewashing/); [ETCC TDS](https://www.scribd.com/document/417661315/Antibak).

- Colloid-type agents were also patented: adding a colloid during cellulase treatment, with "at least 0.4 g colloid per 500 avicelase units" of cellulase — [US 5,885,306](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/5885306); [EP 0665322 A2](https://patents.google.com/patent/EP0665322A2/fr).

### Inferences
- **[INFERENCE]** Mapping of marketing terms to chemistry: "blocker" = fibre-substantive non-ionic polyester (or PVP-type) polymer; "dispersing agent" = fatty alcohol ethoxylate and/or polycarboxylate; "anionic/non-ionic powder" = ethoxylate + polycarboxylate/phosphate on a salt carrier. None of the suppliers uses the terms "PVP" or "polyacrylate" in public pages; these appear only in patents and the academic paper.
- **[INFERENCE]** The dominance of non-ionic declarations is consistent with the academic finding (Section 5) that anionic surfactants inhibit cellulase most strongly; anionic polymer components are confined to patents (SMA hydrolysate, polyacrylic segments) and to powders/clean-up detergents.
- **[INFERENCE]** "Modified polyamide" and "phosphonate" descriptions named in the brief did not appear in any retrieved product document.

### Gaps
- No public document gave EO numbers, alkyl chain lengths, polymer molecular weights (except SMA MW 5,000–20,000 in CN102392374B) or polyester monomer ratios for a *commercial* product.
- The full text of "Nonionic surfactants and dispersants for biopolishing and stonewashing with Hypocrea jecorina cellulases" (journal article surfaced by title only, [catalogue record](https://vufind-test.katalog.k.utb.cz/SummonRecord/FETCH-LOGICAL-c3803-2e44a18bcdc548e69c7d296257557869e43105112abb5e0e4c14db077ce9336b3)) could not be read; it is a likely source of quantitative comparisons.

## 5. Practitioner-reported trade-offs

### Takeaway
The documented trade-offs are: ionic surfactants (especially anionic) inhibit cellulase while non-ionics barely do; efficacy drops in hard water; classical dye-transfer inhibitors work poorly on very hydrophobic indigo; polyester pocketing/elastane needs a dedicated blocker; and newer waterless/low-liquor processes make backstaining worse and ABS chemistry more necessary. Sustainability positioning now centres on ZDHC MRSL Level 3/bluesign/GOTS listings, 100 %-active solids and recycled-PET-based polymers. Quantitative public data on COD load, foam, EIM score or compatibility with ozone/laser/nebulisation were not found.

### Cited Findings
- Enzyme inhibition by surfactant class: anionic surfactants had the largest inhibiting effect on cellulase activity, cationic second, non-ionic little effect — [Yang & Wang, 印染助剂 2012, 29(1): 45–48](https://opaj.napstic.cn/periodicalArticle/0120120615204585).
- A related Chinese study reports that anionic + non-ionic combinations did not give good bio-washing results while non-ionic + non-ionic combinations did — [NAPSTIC preprint record](https://sinoxiv.napstic.cn/article/5408605) (author/year not confirmed).
- One Chinese supplier claims its non-ionic polymer ABS (Sylic CY-N10) *raises* enzyme activity by about 10 % — [nbinno](https://www.nbinno.com/finishing-agents/sylic-cy-n10-denim-washing-anti-dyeing-performance) (supplier claim, unverified).
- Contrast: Seydel markets Seycote SR-100 as "extremely effective as an abrasion enhancement product in providing a high contrast finish without unwanted redeposition" — [Seydel](https://seydel.com/products/garment-washing/anti-redeposition-aids/); IFF/Genencor and Novonesis sell low-backstaining cellulases for "high contrast" looks — [Novonesis](https://www.novonesis.com/en/biosolutions/industrial-bioprocessing/textile/denim-abrasion/denimax-denim-abrasion).
- Cellulase choice matters: patent background notes that Trichoderma cellulases backstain but are preferred for their higher activity on denim, whereas Humicola cellulases generally do not backstain — relayed from the patent set [US 6,251,144](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/6251144) / [US 5,650,322](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/5650322) (exact patent of the quote not pinned down).
- Water hardness: "most of these anti back staining agents are highly efficient in soft water, but in hard water they might be less effective" — [Textile Learner](https://textilelearner.net/?p=11644) (attribution among result set probable); Bozzetto states best efficiency of ANTIDEPOSITUM CPD in soft water — [Scribd](https://www.scribd.com/document/655060465/ANTIDEPOSITUM-CPD-2); polyglycol-ether + polymer-phosphate types are said to cope better with hard water by sequestering metal ions — [Scribd](https://www.scribd.com/document/483501496/Antibackstainingagent).
- Limits of surfactant-only solutions: a patent criticises surfactant-based anti-redeposition for environmental problems and for co-depositing with the dye — [US 5,885,306](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/5885306); conventional dye-transfer-inhibiting polymers are ineffective for indigo because of its extreme hydrophobicity — [US 6,537,327](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/6537327).
- Foam: low-foam is a selling point — Seyco Scour CS-802 "low foaming" ([Seydel](https://seydel.com/products/garment-washing/anti-redeposition-aids/)); Garmon Fortres Flex "non-foaming" ([Garmon (mirror)](https://garmon-mirror.pages.dev/en/textile-chemicals/garment-denim-finishing/dispersing-wetting-agent.html)); polyacrylic-segment polymers chosen because they "do not foam easily" ([CN103233377A](https://patents.google.com/patent/CN103233377A/en)).
- Cost: polyglycol-ether derivatives and enzymatic methods are effective "but more costly"; hydrogen peroxide (0.5–1.0 %) is promoted as "the most cost-effective anti back staining agent", with the caveat that low concentrations are needed to avoid a bleaching effect — [Scribd summary](https://www.scribd.com/document/882058472/Anti-Back-Staining-Agent); [Fibre2Fashion](https://www.fibre2fashion.com/industry-article/3414/a-novel-anti-back-staining-method-for-denim-garments-a-cost-effective-process); [ResearchGate, T. Mehmood, 2019](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/331495368_Anti_Back_Staining_Agent).
- Waterless/low-liquor processes: CHT notes that in its waterless stone-effect process, "without a water bath, indigo adheres strongly to the fibres", causing backstaining on weft threads, inner pockets and labels, hence the dedicated DENIMCOL CLEAN-TPP step — [CHT](https://solutions.cht.com/CHT/WEB.NSF/id/pa_waterless-stone-effect.html).
- Process causes cited by practitioners: unbalanced wash chemistry, incorrect ABS/dispersant dosage, poor liquor ratio and uncontrolled parameters; ABS agents "work as dispersing and sequestering agents, keeping the detached indigo particles suspended in the wash liquor" — [LinkedIn post "Anti-Backstaining: A Small Step…"](https://pk.linkedin.com/in/altafgodil) (low-authority practitioner post).
- Sustainability/compliance: CHT ABS grades carry bluesign APPROVED, GOTS, ZDHC MRSL v3.1 Level 3 and OEKO-TEX suitability ([CHT](https://solutions.cht.com/cht/web.nsf/id/pa_en_productdetail.html?Open=&pID=PDE-RSCR-9LGHWM-EN&prange=WW)); Garmon Fortres Gems is bluesign/ZDHC-listed and its 100 % active solid form is argued to cut logistics and storage costs ([Inside Denim](https://insidedenim.com/News/160465)); Rudolf, ARM Kimya and Seydel offer recycled-PET-derived ABS polymers ([Rudolf](https://insidedenim.com/News/158359); [ARM Kimya](https://www.armkimya.com/anti-back-staining-agents-en1); [Seydel](https://seydel.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/08/Seydel-Eco-line-of-Chemistry-for-Textiles.pdf)); Euro Antibak NBS is declared APEO/NPEO-free ([ETCC TDS](https://www.scribd.com/document/417661315/Antibak)); a Cradle-to-Cradle listing exists for a Bozzetto "dyeing dispersing agent" (denim use not confirmed) ([C2C registry](https://c2ccertified.org/certified-products/dyeing-dispersing-agent)).
- Product-concept trend: ABS function is being built into all-in-one abrasion products (Rudolf RUCOLASE DWS NEW, 2025; IFF IndiAge ONE "upon formulation"; Chinese one-bath "all-purpose enzyme") — [Texintel](https://www.texintel.com/press-room/rudolf-06-25-p-rudolf-group-revolutionises-denim-finishing-with-rucolase-dws-new); [CN108978171B](https://patents.google.com/patent/CN108978171B/zh).

### Inferences
- **[INFERENCE]** The formulation logic that follows: keep the in-bath ABS non-ionic (ethoxylate + non-ionic polyester/PVP) to protect cellulase activity and contrast; put anionic polycarboxylates/phosphates only at levels that do not suppress the enzyme, or reserve them for the hot alkaline clean-up wash where the enzyme is being deactivated anyway.
- **[INFERENCE]** Because ABS agents keep indigo and surfactant in the liquor, they add to effluent COD and colour; public sources reviewed do not quantify this, so any COD claim would need supplier data.
- **[INFERENCE]** Hard-water sensitivity explains why powders commonly include citrate/phosphate builders (cf. citric acid/sodium citrate at 36–45 % of the CN108978171B powder, where it doubles as pH buffer for the neutral cellulase).

### Gaps
- No public data found on: COD/BOD contribution per g/L of ABS agent; foam profiles; compatibility with laccase, ozone (G2), laser or nebulisation/e-flow systems; EIM (Jeanologia) score impact; Cradle-to-Cradle status of specific ABS products; ZDHC Gateway listings for individual Turkish/Indian/Chinese products.
- No quantitative practitioner data on loss of "salt-and-pepper" contrast from over-dosing ABS agents.

## 6. Typical process flow of denim washing and where the ABS agent is dosed

### Takeaway
Public recipes consistently place the ABS agent in (1) the desizing bath with amylase (~60 °C, L:R ~1:9–1:10) and (2) the enzyme/stone abrasion bath (40–60 °C, pH set by cellulase type, L:R ~1:8), with an optional third dose or a dedicated detergent in the rinse/clean-up; suppliers describe their agents as usable "in all treatment steps of jeans washing".

### Cited Findings
- Reference industrial recipe (Textile Learner): desizing at **60 °C, L:R 1:9** (≈ 540 L for the stated batch); then enzyme step at **45 °C, L:R 1:8** (≈ 450 L) with **acid cellulase 2.0 g/l + anti-back-staining agent 0.6 g/l**; cellulase working range 40–60 °C — [Enzyme washing process for dark/medium/light denim](https://textilelearner.net/enzyme-washing-process-for-denim/); [Step-by-step enzyme wash](https://textilelearner.net/step-by-step-enzyme-wash-process/).
- Alternative figures: ABS 0.2–0.3 g/l ([Textile Learner](https://textilelearner.net/enzymes-on-denim-garment-washing/)); neutral enzyme 2 g/L + antibackstainer 1 g/L at lab L:R 1:60 ([Textile Study Center](https://textilestudycenter.com/denim-wet-process-enzyme-wash/)).
- Supplier positioning by step: CHT CLEAN-TFC "for desizing and washing jeans", 0.2–0.5 % in desizing and stonewash ([Scribd TDS](https://www.scribd.com/document/506689723/DENIMCOL-CLEAN-TFC-ficha-tecnica)); Fineotex Finocon MED "in desize, abrasion and clean up, after enzymatic abrasion/bleaching" ([Fineotex](https://www.fineotex.com/textile-chemicals/finocon-med/)); Rudolf DAB/DEJ 220 "in all treatment steps of jeans washing" ([Rudolf](https://www.rudolf-group.it/en/products/textile-auxiliaries/denim/washing-desizing-stonewashing/)); Garmon dispersants keep indigo suspended in stone washing and act as wetters in desizing and bleaching ([Garmon (mirror)](https://garmon-mirror.pages.dev/en/textile-chemicals/garment-denim-finishing/dispersing-wetting-agent.html)); Garmon Fortres LGS/GSL "during all washing steps" (same page).
- Rinse/clean-up stage: older Rudolf guidance — rinse with 2.0 g/l RUCOGEN DEJ depending on backstaining ([pdfcoffee, Rudolf-Info 22/02](https://pdfcoffee.com/jeans-2-pdf-free.html), attribution uncertain); Rudolf Duraner RUCO-TEX DET/OBD powders and Soko BioClean are curative cleaners "for removing back stains on white areas" ([Rudolf Duraner](https://www.rudolf-duraner.com.tr/en/products/textile-auxilaries/pre-treatment/washing-agents-denim/); [Inside Denim](https://insidedenim.com/News/168463)).
- Peroxide variant: hydrogen peroxide 0.5–1.0 % in the mildly alkaline bath acts as ABS (authors attribute backstaining partly to glucose formed during stone/enzyme washing; peroxide releases nascent oxygen) — [Fibre2Fashion](https://www.fibre2fashion.com/industry-article/3414/a-novel-anti-back-staining-method-for-denim-garments-a-cost-effective-process).
- One-bath route: desize + enzyme abrasion + softening merged at 60 °C with liquid enzyme 0.1–0.3 g/L + solid (ABS-containing) enzyme 0.5–1.0 g/L — [CN108978171B](https://patents.google.com/patent/CN108978171B/zh).
- Waterless route: CHT LAB102 — BEIZYM enzymes for abrasion plus DENIMCOL CLEAN-TPP as anti-backstaining/detergent to keep the reverse side clean — [CHT](https://solutions.cht.com/CHT/WEB.NSF/id/pa_waterless-stone-effect.html).
- Continuous (fabric) route: polyester anti-redeposition aid 5–10 g/l on the pad-steamer for flat-finished denim — [Seydel](https://seydel.com/products/textile-finishing/denim/).
- Mechanistic placement rationale: redeposition occurs both in desizing (desorbed dye redeposits on the bleached/white yarn) and in stonewashing — [US 5,885,306](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/5885306).

### Inferences
- **[INFERENCE] Consolidated typical flow** (assembled from the cited fragments; the clean-up conditions in the brief — detergent/soda/peroxide at 60–70 °C — were *not* confirmed by a retrieved source): (1) desize: amylase + wetting/ABS agent ~0.2–0.5 % o.w.g. or ~0.5 g/L, 60 °C, L:R 1:9–1:10 → drain/rinse; (2) abrasion: cellulase (acid pH ~4.5–5.5 or neutral pH 6–8) ± pumice + ABS ~0.5–1.0 g/L (liquid) or 0.2–0.75 % o.w.g. (powder), 40–60 °C, L:R ~1:8, ABS added before/with the enzyme; (3) enzyme deactivation/clean-up wash with detergent (optionally ABS/peroxide); (4) bleach/neutralise as required; (5) soften. Order-of-addition detail (ABS first, then pH buffer, then enzyme) is standard practice in my background knowledge but was not stated in any retrieved source.

### Gaps
- No retrieved source gave time (minutes) for each step with the ABS dose, the order of chemical addition, or the clean-up wash recipe (soda ash/peroxide/detergent, 60–70 °C); these need a full read of the Textile Learner pages, supplier TDS, or R. Paul's *Denim* handbook.
